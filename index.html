Frequently Asked EV Charging Questions

Key engineering benchmarks for home charging efficiency, DC fast taper curves, and battery health.

How much does it cost to charge an EV at home vs. gas?

Adding 45 kWh (20% to 80% on a 75 kWh battery) draws ~49.3 kWh from the wall at 91.3% Level 2 efficiency—costing just $6.90 at $0.14/kWh (or $3.94 on an $0.08/kWh night tariff), compared to ~$19.69 in gas or $21.60 at a $0.48/kWh DC Fast station.

How long does Level 1 vs. Level 2 charging take?

A 120V / 12A Level 1 outlet delivers only ~1.19 kW net to the pack (~85% efficiency), requiring 32–38 hours for a 20%→80% session. A 240V / 32A–40A Level 2 charger (NEMA 14-50) cuts that to 4.7–5.8 hours, easily finishing inside overnight off-peak windows.

Why does DC Fast charging slow down above 80%?

Above 80% SoC, your EV's Battery Management System (BMS) enters the Constant Voltage (CV) taper phase to prevent lithium plating. Charging speed drops to ~40–50 kW average, making 80%→100% take nearly as long as 20%→80%.

How to check EV battery State of Health (SOH)?

Pair a low-power Bluetooth 5.0 BLE OBD2 scanner (such as the OBDLink CX or Veepeak OBDCheck BLE+) with your phone, and log your pack's SOH % and full-charge range in the EV Savings: Charging Log iOS app to export a PDF Battery Health Certificate.
